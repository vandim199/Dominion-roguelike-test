# Persistent bases, junk cards, enemy intents and automatic left-to-right resource consumption

Evidence labels used below: **[opened]** means I read the page itself. **[excerpt]** means I only saw a search-tool excerpt of the page, so wording and attribution are less certain. Rules text is paraphrased throughout.

## Star Realms bases and outposts, and comparable persistent-card systems

### Takeaway
A Star Realms base is a card that leaves the draw cycle and pays out every turn until removed. Destruction costs tempo rather than the card, because the base returns to its owner's discard pile. Outposts make targeting a player-readable rule, but attack values can outgrow defense quickly, so the buying advice I found centres on whether a base survives the next enemy turn.

### Cited Findings
**Star Realms rules**
- Bases are played from hand and stay in play turn after turn until destroyed or scrapped. Buying one only puts it in the discard pile, and the discard phase moves only Ships. — [Rulebook reproduction, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook)
- A base's Defense is the Combat needed to destroy it within a single turn. Paying that amount destroys it and sends it to the owner's discard pile. — [Rulebook reproduction, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook); [official Frontiers rulebook, excerpt](https://files.wisewizardgames.com/starrealms/StarRealms_Frontiers_RuleBook2.pdf)
- While an Outpost is in play, its owner cannot be attacked, and their non-Outpost bases cannot be attacked or targeted, until every Outpost is gone. — [Rulebook reproduction, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook)
- Direct "destroy target base" effects remove a base with no Combat. The rulebook page states the discard destination only for destruction through Combat. — [Rulebook reproduction, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook)
- Base abilities can be used during the owner's main phase. Bases with "or" abilities use one per turn. Ally abilities need another same-faction Ship or Base in play. A scrap ability removes the card permanently. — [Rulebook reproduction, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook)
- Example values: Stellar Reef costs 2 with defense 3, Storage Silo costs 2 with defense 3, and Central Station costs 4 with defense 5 and returns 2 trade. — [Andrea Davis, official Arena article, opened](https://www.starrealms.com/news/star-realms-arena-week-1-rushed-defenses)

**Strategic role and balance**
- Bases gain most from being bought early, and the buying test is whether the opponent can destroy one on their next turn. Bases giving no resources on the turn bought are usually avoided. This advice is for a variant where bases enter play immediately and are scrapped on destruction, so it is not a pure statement about standard play. — [Davis, opened](https://www.starrealms.com/news/star-realms-arena-week-1-rushed-defenses)
- One reviewer puts attack values at roughly 8 to 10 by mid-game, at which point Outposts rarely survive, and calls Outpost survival one of the game's largest flaws. This is a single 2014 opinion. — [Meadow Party, opened](https://meadowparty.com/blog/2014/12/06/star-realms/?amp=1)

**Comparable systems**
- **Ascension constructs:** they stay in play and work every turn, including the turn played. Any number can be played per turn, and each ability is usable once per turn. They have no defense stat. "Destroy" means moving a card from play to the discard pile, and generally refers to constructs. Monster rewards can strip them from opponents. — [Rulebook reproduction, opened](https://rulespal.com/ascension/rulebook)
- **Hero Realms champions:** a champion that takes damage equal to its Defense in one turn is stunned and goes to its owner's discard pile. Damage does not carry between turns. Guards protect the player and non-guard champions while prepared, and guards do not protect other guards. Stun-all effects bypass guards. — [Official rules, excerpt](https://www.herorealms.com/base-game-rules/)
- **Monster Train:** there are three unit floors below a Pyre floor. Enemies climb one floor per turn, floors have a capacity limit (default five per one guide), and cards cost Ember (three per turn). Front-line units are hit first. — [BlueStacks guide, excerpt](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/monster-train/mstn-beginners-guide-en.html); [Steam discussion, excerpt](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1102190/discussions/0/2284960483098962984)
- **Wildfrost:** played companions stay on the field while items go to discard. A damaged companion can be recalled to the discard pile to heal. If the Leader dies, the run ends. — [Community wiki, opened](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Getting_Started)
- **Slay the Spire 2, Necrobinder:** unblocked damage hits the companion Osty before the player, so it works as an extra life bar. Summon raises its HP or revives it, and some cards scale with its HP. — [Blast.tv, opened](https://blast.tv/gaming/news/slay-the-spire-2-all-characters-and-how-to-play-them)
- **Marvel Champions:** Guard is an enemy-side keyword. While a guard minion is engaged with a player, that player cannot attack a villain lacking the keyword. — [Fan keyword list, excerpt](https://hallofheroeslcg.com/marvel-champions-lcg-keyword-list/)

### Inferences
- "Returns to discard" is a soft penalty in all three tabletop systems. The card is delayed, must be redrawn, and in the Coinkeep version must be paid for again. It also means a full board shrinks the deck and a destroyed base re-inflates it, so bases act as temporary thinning.
- Star Realms and Hero Realms reset damage every turn, which makes defense a one-turn threshold. Persistent health bars, as requested, are a different model: they add chip damage, which a threshold system cannot produce, and they need a repair rule. Full repair on returning to discard would be the cheapest rule, but it could make deliberate loss a free heal.
- In the PvP originals, an attacker who hits a base gives up damage to the player. A PvE enemy has no such opportunity cost, so targeting needs an explicit rule to replace that tension.
- Hero Realms ties guard protection to the prepared state, which suggests using a guard can lower protection. That would be a compact use-or-protect trade-off, but I did not confirm the expend rule.

### Gaps
- Whether a Star Realms base's ability can be used the turn it is played was not stated in what I read.
- Search excerpts from official Arena posts mention Fleet HQ at defense 8 and Royal Redoubt at defense 6, and one excerpt calls most bases slow and not worth buying. I could not pin these to a specific article, so treat them as unverified. I found no complete card-defense table.
- No source covered Slay the Spire Powers, Hearthstone minion rules beyond Taunt, or how Marvel Champions allies and consequential damage work.

## Dominion junk, thinning and infinite-turn engines

### Takeaway
Greening works because three things combine: the win currency is dead in hand, hand size is scarce, and a finite supply forces a race. A no-endpoint game loses the race, so the timing tension has to come from somewhere else. The user's complaint matches Dominion's own named pitfall: a deck that draws itself without payload accomplishes little.

### Cited Findings
**Greening**
- Victory cards are usually needed to win but do little during play, so players balance them against cards that keep turns effective. Players start with seven Copper and three Estates and draw five-card hands. The game ends when Provinces run out or any three piles empty. — [Wikipedia, opened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_(card_game))
- The right greening time depends on how resilient an engine is to dead cards and how reliably it hits its target output. Waiting for a reshuffle is usually good play. — [DominionStrategy wiki, excerpt](https://wiki.dominionstrategy.com/index.php/Greening); [When Should I Start Greening?, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2019/03/31/when-should-i-start-greening/)
- Starting Provinces too soon makes an engine sputter and lose, while waiting too long concedes points. — [Dominion 101, opened](https://dominionstrategy.com/2017/11/06/dominion-101-what-is-an-engine/)
- One experienced writer advises erring toward over-building on complex boards, because building preserves flexibility. — [The Value of Building, opened](https://dominionstrategy.com/2021/03/15/the-value-of-building/)

**Designs that make junk useful**
- **Tunnel:** a Victory card that can gain a Gold when discarded outside clean-up, and the reaction rather than the VP is its main value. Tunnel decks end up with more Golds and Victory cards, and discard outlets pair with it. — [Hinterlands: Tunnel, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2012/11/14/hinterlands-tunnel/)
- **Crossroads:** draws one card per Victory card in hand. Strategists note that the Victory cards hurt the deck more than one Crossroads helps, unless they are hybrids or discard-for-benefit cards such as Baron. — [Hinterlands: Crossroads, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2011/10/26/hinterlands-crossroads/)
- **Silk Road:** scores 1 VP per four Victory cards owned. Island/Silk Road reportedly beat Smithy Big Money in 69% of games in one simulation. — [Month of Alt-VP, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2015/11/03/the-month-of-alt-vp-silk-road/)
- **Island:** a hybrid Victory/Action card that is best used to leave the deck quickly. — [DominionStrategy article, excerpt, page not pinned](https://dominionstrategy.com/2011/10/26/hinterlands-crossroads/)
- **Ruins:** unlike Curses, they are worth 0 VP and are themselves Actions, which makes Vineyards and Gardens more viable. — [Cultist, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2018/03/08/cultist/)
- **Death Cart:** it was designed so that its Ruins feel like a benefit to the owner. — [Secret History of Dark Ages, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2012/08/23/the-secret-history-of-dark-ages/)

**Thinning**
- Heavy trashing is the main answer to Ruins floods, and strong trashing can recover from them. — [Marauder, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2013/05/13/dark-ages-marauder/); [Cultist, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2018/03/08/cultist/)
- Engine advice is to trash Estates so they do not interrupt draw, and to keep only a few stop cards. — [Dominion 101, opened](https://dominionstrategy.com/2017/11/06/dominion-101-what-is-an-engine/)

**Engines and loops**
- An engine has draw reaching most or all of the deck, plus payload that makes it worthwhile. A common pitfall is a deck of villages and draw without payload, which can draw itself but has only some coins and one Buy, so it falls behind simple money decks. — [Dominion 101, opened](https://dominionstrategy.com/2017/11/06/dominion-101-what-is-an-engine/)
- A village-and-draw engine should hold at least as many villages as Smithies, according to one simulator result. — [First Game engine, excerpt](https://dominionstrategy.com/2012/07/30/building-the-first-game-engine)
- The Golden Deck is typically five cards (Bishop, Gold, two Silvers, Province) producing the same hand each turn while netting extra buys. It is immune to some attacks, vulnerable to topdecking its Province, and weak against strong non-stalling engines. The wiki flags the page as possibly outdated. — [DominionStrategy wiki, excerpt](https://wiki.dominionstrategy.com/index.php/Golden_Deck)
- Once the game ends, no further turns are played, including extra turns. — [Possession wiki page, excerpt](https://wiki.dominionstrategy.com/index.php/Possession)

### Inferences
- Draw has a hard ceiling at one deck per turn, after which extra draw is worth nothing. The value of a loop therefore comes from what the turn can convert into, which is payload. In Dominion the finite supply also ends the game. Without either limit, an endless turn has nothing to convert into and no end to reach.
- The hand-slot cost of junk exists only while hand size is scarce. A deck that reliably draws everything pays almost nothing for junk, so the junk's tension disappears at exactly the power level where infinite turns appear. A Ward-style card is exposed to this directly.
- Tunnel's clean-up exclusion is the important rule for Ward. A pay-on-discard card with no exclusion pays automatically at end of turn, so its only cost is the slot. With the exclusion, it needs a deliberate discard outlet, which adds a decision and a build requirement.
- With no end-of-game scoring, "necessary junk" needs a different reason to be necessary, because Dominion's reason is scoring. Without a clock there is also no reason to stop building, and even with a clock experienced players lean toward building.
- A deterministic repeating turn (Golden Deck) removes in-turn decisions. That is a plausible cause of hollow-feeling infinites, but it is my reasoning, not a sourced claim.

### Gaps
- I found no source saying Dominion's infinite engines are unsatisfying or pointless. Dominion 101 calls engines rewarding, and I found no Donald X. statement on extra-turn drag. The "hollow infinite" argument above is inference.
- The DominionStrategy wiki is bot-gated, so its Curse, Ruins, Greening and Golden Deck pages were available only as excerpts. I did not read a dedicated Curse article, and the -1 VP value was not confirmed in what I read.
- Seaside Durations and Adventures Reserve cards, which are close precedents for temporary persistence, were not researched.

## Slay the Spire enemies, block, poison, energy and Slay the Spire 2

### Takeaway
Showing intent is what turned Slay the Spire's random move lists into a game of decisions, according to its developers. Infinite loops are handled by per-fight rules on specific enemies rather than by global bans, and Slay the Spire 2's first large balance patch made them harder to set up without removing them.

### Cited Findings
**Intent**
- Mega Crit's Anthony and Casey said "it was revealing the enemy's intent that finally made Slay the Spire come together." Before that, enemies picked randomly from move lists, and an earlier next-move display was cluttered and hard to read in multi-enemy fights. Players often could not tell whether to block or whether it was safe to attack. This is from an AI-generated summary of the Ars Technica War Stories transcript, so verify against the video. — [Readray transcript summary, opened](https://readray.ai/notes/1046045)
- Icons encode the plan: a shield for block, and a sword with a shield behind it for a hybrid move. — [Readray transcript summary, opened](https://readray.ai/notes/1046045)
- Slay the Spire 2 keeps the system, and one guide describes it as offering nearly full information. — [Untapped guide, excerpt](https://sts2.untapped.gg/guides/how-to-read-enemy-intent)

**Metrics**
- Giovannetti's GDC 2019 talk covers metrics-driven balancing. — [GDC listing, excerpt](https://gdconf.com/news/learn-slay-spires-successful-metrics-driven-approach-game-balancing-gdc-2019)
- A third-party summary of the talk says enemies were designed alongside cards, data came from anonymous end-of-run reports, and metrics can mislead. Its example is Madness, which looked overpowered because it was acquired late. — [BriefRead, opened](https://briefread.pages.dev/en-GB/summary/slay-the-spire-metrics-driven-design)

- Poison triggers at the start of the enemy's turn, ignores Block, deals its stack in HP loss, then drops by 1. — [Poison, opened](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Poison)
- Catalyst multiplies existing Poison instead of applying new stacks. — [Poison, opened](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Poison)

**Anti-infinite rules**
- Time Eater ends the player's turn and gains Strength every 12 cards. — [Fandom excerpt](https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Infinite_Combo); [slaythespire.gg boss notes, opened](https://slaythespire.gg/bosses)
- The Heart punishes each card played through Beat of Death and caps damage per turn through Invincible, so the fastest kill takes 3 turns. — [Search excerpt of a Heart guide](https://www.highgroundgaming.com/slay-the-spire-corrupt-heart-guide/)
- Slay the Spire 2 patch 0.100.0 (beta, March 20, 2026) made infinites harder to set up but did not remove them. Prepared now costs energy and no longer draws. Stoke and Spite lost their draw. Expect a Fight blocks further energy gain that turn. Regent's Alignment rose to three stars. The article's framing of infinites as part of the power curve is the writer's, not a Mega Crit quote. — [PCGamesN, opened](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/patch-notes-infinites-nerfed)

**Slay the Spire 2 status and additions**
- Early Access began March 5, 2026. — [One More Game, excerpt](https://onemoregame.ph/?p=144488)
- Listed new systems include Alternate Acts, Enchantments, Quest cards, Afflictions and Ancients, with more content planned. — [Early-access guide, excerpt](https://stratgg.pages.dev/guides/early-access/)
- The Regent's Blue Stars are a second resource alongside energy, and some cards need both. The Necrobinder's Doom kills an enemy that ends its turn at or below a marked HP value, regardless of Block. Defect orbs return. — [Blast.tv, opened](https://blast.tv/gaming/news/slay-the-spire-2-all-characters-and-how-to-play-them)

### Inferences
- Intent converts a damage race into a per-turn allocation problem, because the player knows the size of the need before spending. Automatic systems benefit even more, since a board that acts without input can only be planned around if the threat is known in advance.
- Hexaghost's Divider scales with the player's current HP. A healthy player takes the biggest hit, so a high-HP buffer alone is not safe. Rules that read the player's own state are a route to scaling pressure without inflating everything.
- Status cards attack the deck's generator role. They reduce effective hand size temporarily and vanish after combat, so the pressure does not permanently punish a long-term deck. In a game with a permanent deck, that distinction matters.
- Poison's useful property is that it is a clock that bypasses block and pays over time. That suits a charge-and-fire board because it rewards early firing, unlike burst damage, which only needs a final threshold.
- The Slay the Spire 2 patch mostly removed draw and energy refunds from individual cards. That points to the usual fix for infinites: break the closure of the loop rather than the cards' main effect.

### Gaps
- I did not reach a complete HP and damage table. Numbers above are samples, and I have no sourced figure for Act 2 and Act 3 normal enemies or for starting player HP.
- From memory only, not verified this session: Block expires at the start of the player's next turn, and three energy per turn is the default.
- The wiki's Hexaghost page conflicts with itself on Inferno damage (24 in the hints, against 2×6 in the table), so I omitted that move's damage.
- Slay the Spire 2 information is early-access and may have changed: the newest sources I reached date from about March to April 2026, against today's date of November 2026. Sources conflict on the launch roster. Excerpts from community guides give Ceremonial Beast 252, Vantom 173 and Knowledge Demon 379 HP, and 750 or 800 for its Heart, but I could not pin those to a specific page.
- PC Gamer's excerpt says the Early Access length is unknown, but the full article would not load.

## Automatic-trigger and ordered boards

### Takeaway
Order matters only where something does not commute: arithmetic, adjacency, a finite flow, or exposure to attack. Nearly all of these games move decisions out of the fight and into composition and placement between fights.

### Cited Findings
**The Bazaar**
- Players acquire items that act automatically in battle. Items take one, two or three board slots, and layout is central. — [PC Gamer, excerpt](https://pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/the-bazaar-could-be-the-future-of-autobattlers-if-it-stops-strangling-itself-to-death-with-its-own-microtransactions)
- Position acts through adjacency: some items cut neighbours' cooldowns, and others haste them. — [Z-Sword](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/33f5vo2wj32lumw5zfm68ihs6/Z-Sword), [Captain's Wheel](https://bazaardb.gg/card/66lc1mkru0sat18gqibh8wfry/Captain's-Wheel) (excerpts; patch-dependent)
- Some effects reference left or right directly. — [Powder Horn](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/8aqvhhs8zv1n2f0bf3mqhiwz/Powder-Horn), [Jury Rigger](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/7naxategvhxmqkkq0bkt7vf1/Jury-Rigger) (excerpts)
- A guide says triggers pass through a hidden internal cooldown, and that a few items can exceed it and reach about 60 uses per second. — [Mobalytics, excerpt, page not pinned](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/builds/fiery-pufferfish-rarran)
- Reynad has talked about shifting passive items toward active ones. This comes from a secondary summary. — [Bazaar Builds, excerpt](https://bazaar-builds.net/reynad-interview-insights-on-the-future-of-the-game/)

**Backpack Battles**
- Items check neighbouring cells marked with stars, and rotation matters. — [Casual Game Guides, excerpt](https://casualgameguides.com/walkthroughs/backpack-battles/backpack-layout-item-positioning)
- Weapons drain a shared stamina pool, and more weapons drain it faster. Running out costs attacks, and long fights can add fatigue damage. These are community guides that may lag patches. — [Gamepressure, excerpt](https://www.gamepressure.com/newsroom/backpack-battles-guide-tips-for-beginners/ze5cbd)

**Balatro**
- Jokers generally resolve left to right, so additive multiplier belongs left of multiplicative. — [Destructoid, excerpt](https://www.destructoid.com/how-to-order-jokers-in-balatro/)
- Exceptions exist: jokers triggering when a card is scored fire during card scoring. — [Strategy guide, excerpt, page not pinned](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/balatro-tips-for-scoring-big)
- Blueprint copies the joker on its right. — [Source Gaming, opened](https://sourcegaming.info/2024/11/15/holism-breaking-balatro/)
- One analysis treats synergy rather than order as the main appeal, and points to drawback jokers and money as limits on runaway power. — [Source Gaming, opened](https://sourcegaming.info/2024/11/15/holism-breaking-balatro/)

**Super Auto Pets**
- Teams are arranged in the shop, and combat has no positioning decisions. Front pets fight until one faints, and the next moves up. — [Community wiki stub, opened](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Basics)
- Ability order may follow attack strength, according to another guide. — [Gameplay Tips, excerpt](https://gameplay.tips/guides/9959-super-auto-pets-strategy-guide.html)

**Hearthstone Battlegrounds**
- Left minions attack first, and targets are random unless the opponent has a Taunt minion. — [Blizzard announcement, excerpt](https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-gb/news/23156373)
- Common advice includes ordering by attack, and avoiding Taunt on cleave units so they do not die before attacking. — [Icy Veins, excerpt](https://www.icy-veins.com/hearthstone/hearthstone-battlegrounds-strategy-tips)

**Wildfrost**
- Counters tick with enemies first, then top-to-bottom and front-to-back on each field. The player plays one card per turn or uses a redraw bell. — [Community wiki, opened](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Getting_Started)

### Inferences
- There are four sources of positional meaning: non-commuting operations (Balatro), neighbour effects (The Bazaar, Backpack Battles), scarce flow (Backpack Battles stamina), and exposure (Hearthstone Taunt, front lines). A left-to-right ammo board gets only the third for free, and only while ammo is scarce.
- Balatro's core ordering rule is close to solved, which is acceptable because positional jokers give it exceptions. A simple left-first feed risks the same outcome without those exceptions.
- Automatic games move decisions to the shop. Coinkeep differs because players also act in combat, so the board's automation needs to create hand decisions rather than replace them.
- The Bazaar's internal trigger cap, if accurate, shows that auto-trigger games need a throttle somewhere, and that exceptions to it are where degenerate builds concentrate.

### Gaps
- I found no statement from LocalThunk on why order was designed this way, and no direct Reynad statement on adjacency or stalemate rules. A low-quality mirror says The Bazaar began as a deckbuilder and dropped decks, which I could not verify.
- Sources conflict on Super Auto Pets' ability order and on how to describe attack direction, so test in-game if it matters.
- Mechabellum was not researched.

## Feed-and-fire and charge mechanics

### Takeaway
Direct precedents exist for each piece, but I found no game combining all of them. Orbs show overflow as a payout, The Bazaar shows ammo as a throttle, and Factorio shows the exact starvation risk of a first-in-line feed. In a left-to-right system, overflow and refill rules matter more than any other choice.

### Cited Findings
- **Defect orbs:** slots run first-in, first-out. Channeling with full slots Evokes the oldest orb first to make room, so overflow pays out rather than being lost. The Defect starts with 3 slots, with a maximum of 10. Most passives fire at end of turn, and Plasma's at start of turn. The wiki's intro and mechanics sections differ on slot wording. — [Orbs, opened](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Orbs)
- Lightning hits a random enemy, while Dark targets lowest HP, with ties going leftmost. Focus raises most orb output and can go negative, and enemies can reduce it. — [Orbs, opened](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Orbs)
- **The Bazaar:** an item out of Ammo stops charging, and Ammo refills after each fight. Charge advances a cooldown immediately. Reload adds Ammo only to items with a maximum. — [Bazaar Builds keyword list, excerpt](https://bazaar-builds.net/the-bazaar-essential-keywords-and-terms-explained-for-gamers/)
- Reload appears globally, on neighbours, and on the item to one side. — [Port](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/1ehgyl09o0z1hqi8cxidiev87/Port), [Ramrod](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/eyxe53xnbv41r8bfkca3q2g7o/Ramrod), [Powder Horn](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/8aqvhhs8zv1n2f0bf3mqhiwz/Powder-Horn) (excerpts; patch-dependent)
- **Wildfrost:** each card's counter ticks down each turn. At zero the card acts and resets to its maximum. Snow stops the countdown, and some effects trigger a card early. — [Counter, excerpt](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Counter); [Getting Started, opened](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Getting_Started)
- **Aeon's End:** focusing a closed breach costs aether and allows preparing a spell to it. Each breach holds one spell. Spells in closed breaches must be cast next turn, while open breaches may hold them. The turn-order deck is shuffled, but the player deck is not. — [Fan wiki, excerpt](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Breach); [Rules summary, excerpt](https://rulespal.com/aeons-end/rulebook); [VGDB, excerpt](https://www.vgdb.co/games/aeons-end)
- **Cobalt Core:** you spend energy on cards including evasion, weighing offence against defence each turn. Moving out of a cannon's path dodges it. The midrow holds obstacles, missiles and drones, and can block attacks. Ship parts have traits such as Armor and Brittle. Temporary shields expire at the start of your turn. — [Card Gamer, opened](https://cardgamer.com/games/digital-card-games/cobalt-core-is-a-cozy-space-deckbuilder-with-a-lot-of-charm/); [Starter guide, opened](https://gamepretty.com/cobalt-core-starting-guide-for-new-players/)
- **Factorio:** players report that during long attacks the first turret on a belt can take nearly all ammo, leaving later turrets dry. Buffer chests and direct pickup are the workarounds. These are forum opinions, not official mechanics. — [Factorio forum, excerpt](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=396127)
- **Regent:** cards can cost energy and stars together, and the starter relic grants 3 stars per combat. — [Blast.tv, opened](https://blast.tv/gaming/news/slay-the-spire-2-all-characters-and-how-to-play-them)

### Inferences
- The refill rule decides whether left-first is starvation or scheduling. Example with 6 ammo per turn, a left base costing 3, and a right base costing 10:
  - If a base can refill and fire repeatedly in one feed, the left base fires twice per turn and the right base gets nothing, forever.
  - If each base can fire once per turn, the left base takes 3 and the right base gets 3 per turn. The right base fires on turn 4, taking only 1 that turn, and 2 ammo overflows.
- Carry-over of partial fill weakens thresholds. If partial ammo persists and nothing is urgent, long-run output is roughly ammo multiplied by payoff per ammo, so the solved order is best ratio first. Order stays interesting only when something breaks that: deadlines from enemy intents, a per-turn firing limit that saturates the top base, buffs that must fire before payloads, or enemies targeting positions.
- Without carry-over, hard thresholds make the feed a small knapsack problem and greedy ordering can fail. With 6 ammo, one base costing 4 and paying 8 loses to two bases costing 3 and paying 5 each, despite its better ratio. The cost is that wasted partial fills may feel bad.
- Overflow has four possible treatments: lost, passed along, banked in a capped pool, or converted to a weak payout. Orbs show why converted overflow feels good. Loss is the simplest rule, and it is the one that creates a reason not to overproduce.
- Ammo as a throttle is also an anti-infinite tool. If base firing is limited by per-turn or per-fight ammo, an extra-long turn can only spend what the deck generates, and the Bazaar's Reload shows how that limit can become a build target of its own.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm Cobalt Core's exact energy, heat or telegraph rules, or Into the Breach's enemy action-order rule. From memory only: Into the Breach displays enemy resolution order.
- Mindustry and other factory-feed games were not confirmed. Factorio evidence is forum-level.
- No source discussed overflow as an explicit design topic, so the overflow taxonomy above is my own.

## Protecting a board: interesting, legible targeting

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence favours deterministic rules plus advance warning. Randomness is tolerable on small effects, but a loss the player could not foresee is what these designs set out to remove.

### Cited Findings
- Into the Breach's Justin Ma says the team wanted less randomness than FTL, with clear rulesets, so that each death would feel self-inflicted. That goal led to telegraphed attacks as a core mechanic, which makes the game feel like a puzzle. — [Game Developer interview, opened](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)
- Guard-style rules are binary and readable: Outposts shield their owner and other bases, and Hero Realms guards shield the owner and non-guard champions but not one another. — [Star Realms rules, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook); [Hero Realms rules, excerpt](https://www.herorealms.com/base-game-rules/)
- Those shields have gaps: direct-destroy effects skip combat in Star Realms, and stun-all effects get past Hero Realms guards. — [Star Realms rules, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook); [Hero Realms rules, excerpt](https://www.herorealms.com/base-game-rules/)
- Battlegrounds uses random targets unless a Taunt is present, so Taunt doubles as the only way to steer attacks. — [Blizzard, excerpt](https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-gb/news/23156373)
- Wildfrost attacks hit the frontmost card in the opposite row, and keywords change this. — [Getting Started, opened](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Getting_Started)
- Wildfrost players can recall a damaged companion to the discard pile to heal it, the Leader excepted, and a Wave Bell shows when reinforcements arrive. — [Getting Started, opened](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Getting_Started)
- Wildfrost's Clunkers use Scrap rather than Health, so any hit of 1 or more removes one Scrap regardless of size. — [Getting Started, opened](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Getting_Started)
- Monster Train units take hits front-first, but some back-line enemies teleport upward, which challenges builds tuned for the front. — [Community or review source, excerpt, page not pinned](https://rogueliker.com/monster-train-review/)
- Cobalt Core lets players dodge by moving out of the path of cannons, and damage lands on specific ship parts with different traits. — [Starter guide, opened](https://gamepretty.com/cobalt-core-starting-guide-for-new-players/); [Card Gamer, opened](https://cardgamer.com/games/digital-card-games/cobalt-core-is-a-cozy-space-deckbuilder-with-a-lot-of-charm/)
- Star Realms' reported failure: attack grows faster than defense, so Outposts stop surviving by mid-game. — [Meadow Party, opened](https://meadowparty.com/blog/2014/12/06/star-realms/?amp=1)

### Inferences
- Legible targeting needs a one-sentence default rule and an intent that shows the target and amount before the player spends energy. Dark orbs' lowest-HP-then-leftmost rule is a model of a deterministic rule that can still be played around.
- An attack is only interesting if the player has a response. Possible responses found in these games are shielding, reordering, sacrificing or recalling, killing first, and firing early. If none are available on a given turn, the intent is only a warning.
- The rule for excess damage matters. It should be stated whether damage past a base's health is lost, carries to the next target, or hits the player. Absorption makes low-health bases into shields, while carry-over reduces that exploit.
- Monster Train and Wildfrost both use a rule where the front unit gets hit. Combined with a left-first ammo feed, the same position could both receive ammo first and take hits first, which creates a real placement trade-off. If enemies hit from the opposite side, the two systems stop interacting, and placement solves itself.
- Scrap-style durability is a useful anti-scaling tool: a base counting hits rather than damage cannot be one-shot by large numbers. In a game with big numbers, this protects against the Star Realms failure. It also makes multi-hit attacks a natural counter.
- Death spirals are the main board-specific risk: lost bases reduce output, replaying costs energy, and the same pressure continues. Discard return softens the permanent loss but not the tempo loss, so a recovery path needs checking.
- Unavoidable random loss comes from a stack of three things: a random target, damage above the target's health, and no response available. Removing any one prevents it.

### Gaps
- I found no study or developer talk specifically on enemy targeting of a player's persistent board. The recommendations above are inference from adjacent systems.
- Hearthstone's constructed Taunt rule was not separately sourced, and only the Battlegrounds description was checked.

## Keeping hand and board non-redundant, and likely failure modes

### Takeaway
The generator and consumer split works only if energy stays scarce enough that the hand keeps making real choices. Each failure mode the user is worried about has a precedent, and most are visible in the loop's closure: what refills what, and what limits it.

### Cited Findings
- Monster Train limits units through two independent budgets, Ember and floor capacity. — [BlueStacks guide, excerpt](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/monster-train/mstn-beginners-guide-en.html)
- Wildfrost's hand mixes units that stay and items that discard, and the player gets one play per turn. — [Getting Started, opened](https://wildfrostwiki.com/Getting_Started)
- Aeon's End charges aether for both buying and preparing, and casting is delayed by a turn, so hand and board compete for one resource and for time. — [Fan wiki, excerpt](https://aeonsend.wiki.gg/wiki/Breach)
- Star Realms ally abilities require another same-faction card in play, so bases keep allies active across turns, and hand and board reinforce one another. — [Rulebook reproduction, opened](https://rulespal.com/star-realms/rulebook)
- Super Auto Pets confines positioning to the shop phase. — [Community wiki, opened](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Basics)
- Dominion's engine advice is to keep few non-drawing stop cards and to avoid draw without payload. — [Dominion 101, opened](https://dominionstrategy.com/2017/11/06/dominion-101-what-is-an-engine/)
- Slay the Spire 2's anti-infinite patch targeted draw and energy refunds, and raised a second-resource cost. — [PCGamesN, opened](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/patch-notes-infinites-nerfed)
- Mega Crit's metrics approach logged cards, enemies and choices at run end, and warned that late-acquired cards can look stronger than they are. — [BriefRead summary, opened](https://briefread.pages.dev/en-GB/summary/slay-the-spire-metrics-driven-design)

### Inferences
**Keeping the two roles distinct**
- A hand card and a base with the same effect differ only in timing, so the differences need to be systematic: hand cards answer this turn's intent, and bases deliver larger, slower, scheduled output while exposed to attack.
- Ammo cards with no alternative use are autoplay. If the hand always plays everything, the deck effectively plays itself. Energy, or an alternative use for an ammo card, is what forces a choice.
- If the same cards generate ammo, draw and energy, the loop can close on itself. Dominion and Slay the Spire 2 both suggest watching for draw and energy refunds on cheap cards.

**Likely failure modes**

| Failure | How it shows up | Precedent | Pressure against it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solved order | Best ratio always leftmost | Balatro's additive-before-multiplicative rule; carry-over analysis above | Intent deadlines, per-turn firing limits, position-based attacks, neighbour effects |
| One dominant base | Left base eats all ammo, or one effect wins | Factorio's first-turret monopoly | Once-per-turn firing, diminishing returns, enemies that punish it, board limits |
| Deck plays itself | Every hand is played in full; no choice remains | Autobattlers putting decisions in the shop | Energy scarcity, intent responses, ammo alternatives |
| Hollow infinite | Long loop with no meaningful output | Dominion draw without payload | Loop-closure limits, finite ammo, payload scaling, enemy rules that read card counts |
| Junk stops costing | Hand size no longer binds, so junk is free | Draw-your-deck engines | Cost tied to something beyond hand slots, such as discard-only payoff with an exclusion |
| Unwinnable board | Attack outgrows base health | Star Realms Outposts | Scaling health, hit-count durability, repair by cycling |
| Death spiral | Lost bases reduce output, replays cost energy | Inferred from discard return rules | Cheap redeploys, partial output, intent warnings |
| Trigger overload | Too many rapid triggers, on-screen or in logic | The Bazaar's internal cooldown, and items that exceed it | Trigger caps, batched resolution |
| Misleading balance data | A strong card looks weak or vice versa | Madness metric artefact | Compare by point of acquisition |

**Implication for the user's complaint**
- An infinite turn only matters if the board has something to convert it into, and if there is a per-turn limit preventing it from being trivially maximal. Separating ammo generation from the player's action count could help, since then extra actions only matter when they raise real output rather than repeat a closed loop.

### Gaps
- I found no published design discussion or postmortem of a game that uses a deck to feed a board in this way. This is the main evidence gap, because the combination is original, and the failure table rests on analogy.
- BoardGameGeek design threads and r/slaythespire and r/deckbuildinggames discussions were not reached in any depth, so player-sentiment evidence is thin.
- Whether these rules hold up in Coinkeep needs simulation or playtesting, and none was run for these notes.
