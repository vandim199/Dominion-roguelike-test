# Hybrid game precedents: deckbuilding and tableau games meeting incremental progression

Research date: 3 November 2026. Source labels used below:
- **Vaporlens** pages are AI-generated summaries of Steam reviews. Their 0-to-1 weights show relative mention frequency, not percentages, and they are sometimes internally inconsistent. Treat them as directional evidence of player sentiment, not as measurements.
- **Snippet-level** means the claim came from a search result summary and the page itself was not read in full.
- All pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so wording is paraphrased.

## What is "Chef Knight"?

### Takeaway
Chef Knight is a real, unambiguous match: a roughly 3-hour active incremental game by Clover Bite, released on Steam on 1 June 2026, and it is **not a deckbuilder and has no cards**. Its appeal comes from a timed fight, cook, sell and upgrade loop, with income rising from hundreds to quadrillions over one short, finite game.

### Cited Findings

#### Identity
- Title "Chef Knight", developer and publisher Clover Bite, Steam app 4304930, release date 1 June 2026, price 6.15 EUR, tagged Incremental, Idler, Resource Management, Cooking, Action and others. English reviews stood at Overwhelmingly Positive, 95% of 1,571, at the time of the snapshot — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4304930/Chef_Knight/)
- Date conflict: the Steam page says 1 June, while a Japanese outlet reporting before launch gave 2 June, probably a time zone difference (my inference). The same outlet names the creator as Amir, says the game was inspired by the anime Delicious in Dungeon, and says it was meant as a relaxed incremental — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20260512-442485/)
- Clover Bite also made Grime, Grime II and Slingbot Survivors. Developer's length estimate is about three hours, with four environments, 12 monster types and 16 dishes. It is designed for fast progression, not a long campaign — [Vandal](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia-amp/1350790799/este-juego-incremental-se-inspira-en-tragones-y-mazmorras-y-promete-una-experiencia-intensa-de-tres-horas/)
- A Steam discussion thread is titled around reaching 100% in 2.6 hours — [Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4304930/discussions/). Reviewers commonly report 3 to 3.5 hours — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4304930/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&p=1), [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4304930/chef_knight.md)

#### Core loop
- Enter a top-down dungeon, kill monsters for ingredients, cook them in a side-view kitchen, sell dishes for gold, then spend gold on a large skill tree that affects combat, weapons, exploration and cooking. Kitchen equipment and hired helpers unlock more valuable dishes over time — [Vandal](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia-amp/1350790799/este-juego-incremental-se-inspira-en-tragones-y-mazmorras-y-promete-una-experiencia-intensa-de-tres-horas/)
- Dungeon sorties are time-limited. Time is awarded as you progress, you advance by meeting a quota, and you destroy a large chest to clear an area. Green gems come from chests — [Vulgar Knight](https://vulgarknight.com/chef-knight-review/)
- A small corner indicator shows progress toward the door to the next floor, upgrades lengthen the timer, and red gems come from per-run quests. Missing a recipe scroll loses it for that run — [Gamescreed](https://www.gamescreed.com/reviews/chef-knight-review)
- Cooking a set number of certain dishes unlocks new recipes and the next floor, so kitchen output gates dungeon depth — [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/20260512-442485/)
- Skill-tree examples include damage, attack cone width, movement speed, cooking and selling speed, enemy drop rate, timer length and dish value, plus passive chain lightning — [Gamescreed](https://www.gamescreed.com/reviews/chef-knight-review)
- Conflict on structure: one review says each dungeon has three stages — [Vulgar Knight](https://vulgarknight.com/chef-knight-review/); another says four layers per area — [Gamescreed](https://www.gamescreed.com/reviews/chef-knight-review)

#### How the power curve feels
- Earnings grow from hundreds to quadrillions. You become overpowered in one dungeon, then the next one restarts the challenge, which this reviewer treats as part of the appeal. Upgrades make a clear difference and no paths are locked, so you can eventually unlock everything — [Vulgar Knight](https://vulgarknight.com/chef-knight-review/)
- Early floors are gentle, and cook and sell times shrink to a level the reviewer finds ridiculous and fun. At very high power the attack cone can wrap around the player, which makes combat feel worse — [Gamescreed](https://www.gamescreed.com/reviews/chef-knight-review)
- Some players found the very start slow, including needing several attempts on the first floor before progression improved — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4304930/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&p=1)
- The dissenting view on the curve: upgrade costs jump erratically, gains feel offset by rising costs, progress can stall, the tree offers little real choice or planning, and one reviewer says automation makes money irrelevant near the end — [Steam negative reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4304930/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&p=1)

#### What players praise and criticise
- Praise by weight: progression and upgrades 0.82, addictive loop 0.65, charming visuals 0.64, short but balanced length 0.62, value 0.56, pacing 0.56. Criticism: too short 0.97, chaotic final level 0.54, balance and low tension 0.46, visual and audio bugs 0.28, limited variety 0.11. Players compare the combat feel to Vampire Survivors — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4304930/chef_knight.md)
- Severe slowdown in the final stages, reportedly about 2 FPS, repetition across four biomes, and hazards that pose little challenge — [Vulgar Knight](https://vulgarknight.com/chef-knight-review/)
- Late-game visual clutter hides the character, the aiming cone or the mouse — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4304930/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&p=1)
- Conflict on danger: one source says the threat comes from enemy numbers and requires precise aim and dodging — [Vandal](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia-amp/1350790799/este-juego-incremental-se-inspira-en-tragones-y-mazmorras-y-promete-una-experiencia-intensa-de-tres-horas/); another says enemies do not damage you on early floors — [Gamescreed](https://www.gamescreed.com/reviews/chef-knight-review); a negative reviewer says there is little to dodge — [Steam negative reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4304930/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&p=1)
- Conflict on post-game: negative reviewers say there is no endless mode or new game plus — [Steam negative reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4304930/negativereviews/?browsefilter=toprated&p=1); a reviewer says play can continue after the story ends — [Gamescreed](https://www.gamescreed.com/reviews/chef-knight-review)

### Inferences
- The user's only reference is a finite, short, active game where power is mostly a sequence to complete, not a set of strategic bets. "No locked paths" means the tree controls pacing more than it creates build identity. A deckbuilder gets its value from exclusive choices, so Chef Knight may set expectations about feel but offers little about decision design.
- The main things Chef Knight demonstrates are a short timed sortie, an immediate return to a shop, one short "too strong now" stretch before the next zone resets the challenge, and a game that ends before the loop tires. "Too short" is its top complaint, yet it holds about 95% positive, so leaving players wanting more did little harm at this price.
- Its peak moments are also where it breaks: clutter, a cone that wraps around the player, and heavy frame-rate loss. Those failures appear exactly when numbers are largest, which is a warning for any hybrid aiming at trillions.

### Gaps
- I could not find the length of a single sortie in seconds, or a measured time between upgrades. No source quantifies how often a power spike lands.
- A rebirth system is mentioned at low weight (0.14) by [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4304930/chef_knight.md), but no other source describes one and no discussion title refers to it. Treat it as unconfirmed.
- Reddit threads could not be read, because reddit.com and the mirrors I tried were unreachable. Player opinion here comes from Steam reviews, aggregators and critic reviews.

## Which released games explicitly combine deckbuilding and incremental/idle progression?

### Takeaway
Very few do, and those that do are small: Feed The Void (free prototype, Steam version pending), Card Storm Idle (Mixed reviews), Mine Cards (a game jam entry), Retromine (itch.io, Steam version pending), plus Orc Incremental, which uses card drafting rather than a deck. Most famous names on the requested list (Balatro, The Bazaar, Backpack Battles and others) are roguelikes or auto-battlers, not incrementals, and Nodebuster is an incremental with no cards.

### Inferences
- The explicit hybrid space has a few small entries and no breakout hit that I could find. Feed The Void's 597 ratings at 4.8/5 suggest demand, though it is a free prototype, so that demand is unproven at a paid price.
- "Balatro-like" and "incremental" are often blurred in casual discussion. Balatro's base target at the winning ante is only 50,000 before blind multipliers, and no power persists between runs, so it delivers the *feeling* of big numbers without the incremental structure of long-term growth.
- Two 2026 launches (Grail, Orc Incremental) and two pending Steam versions (Feed The Void, Retromine) suggest this space is getting busier right now. Any gap identified below may narrow soon.

## For the most relevant games: what repeats, what scales, what persists, what is automated, and how long is a session or spike?

### Takeaway
Spend decisions sit between short automated bursts in almost every game here: 10-to-20-second fights in Backpack Battles, and timed sorties in Chef Knight. True incrementals keep permanent power, while the roguelikes keep only unlocks. Outside Balatro's endless mode, only Chef Knight and Orc Incremental are documented reaching very large numbers, and in the card-based games checked, normal play stays far below trillions.

### Cited Findings

#### Balatro
- **Repeats:** play poker hands to reach a target score across three blinds per ante, then visit a shop — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro)
- **Scales:** score is Chips times Mult, and Jokers add or multiply. Base targets run 300, 800, 2,000, 5,000, 11,000, 20,000, 35,000, 50,000 for antes 1 to 8, then endless runs 110,000, 560,000, 7.2M, 300M, 47B, 2.9e13, 7.7e16, 8.6e20 for antes 9 to 16. Big Blind is 1.5x and most Boss Blinds 2x. Ante 39 overflows to naneinf, ending the run — [Balatro Wiki](https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes)
- **My calculation from that table:** step sizes shrink through the main game (2.67x down to 1.43x per ante, 167x overall), then accelerate in endless (2.2x, 5.1x, 12.9x, 41.7x, up to about 11,000x). Trillions are first crossed at ante 14, six antes into endless.
- **Number cap:** the base game stops near 10^308. The Talisman mod extends this — [Talisman](https://github.com/Dimserene/Talisman/)
- **Order:** Jokers generally trigger left to right, so +Mult belongs to the left of xMult, and placement is a real decision — [Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/how-to-order-jokers-in-balatro/)
- **Persists:** unlocks of decks, stakes and cards only. Planet upgrades and deck changes do not carry over — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balatro)
- **Automated:** score resolution after you choose a hand. **Session:** about 1.5 hours, first win about 10 hours — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2379780/balatro.md)

#### The Bazaar
- **Repeats:** a run spans in-game days split into hours. Each hour offers three choices among merchants, monster fights and events, with a fight against another player's ghost at day's end — [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/the-bazaar-game-review-open-beta)
- **Scales:** item stats and upgrades. Rewards scale with wins, capped at 10, and losing costs Prestige — [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/the-bazaar-game-review-open-beta)
- **Automated:** combat. Items fire on cooldown timers, which Slow, Haste, Charge and Freeze alter (snippet-level) — [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/the-bazaar-gameplay-and-story-info), [Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/keywords-and-terms)
- **Persists:** the mid-run board is saved and can be resumed — [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/the-bazaar-game-review-open-beta). **Session:** no timers between fights, so players can pause freely — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1617400/the_bazaar.md)

#### Backpack Battles
- **Repeats:** up to 18 rounds of shop then battle. A run is won at 10 wins and lost at 5 losses — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpack_Battles)
- **Scales:** rarer items in later rounds, adjacency effects, and recipes that combine adjacent items after battles — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpack_Battles)
- **Automated:** combat, with items triggering on timers and neighbours. **Spike:** battles usually last 10 to 20 seconds — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpack_Battles). **Session:** about 20 to 30 minutes per match — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2427700/backpack_battles.md)

#### Grail
- **Repeats:** build a deck between fights, then the deck plays itself. Runs begin with three cards — [CardGamer](https://cardgamer.com/reviews/grail-review/)
- **Scales:** energy starts at 4 and rises by 1 per turn with a draw. An empty deck costs 2 turns to reshuffle. Statuses trigger off deck events: Burn on discard, Poison on shuffle, Shock on energy gain, Bleed on damage. Stickers can rig order — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3597200/grail.md)
- **Persists:** characters and cosmetics via trophies. **Session:** 15 to 45 minutes per run — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3597200/grail.md). Battle speed is adjustable — [CardGamer](https://cardgamer.com/reviews/grail-review/)

#### Orc Incremental
- **Repeats:** earn gold in auto-battles, buy card draws (units or totems), set a formation, push, then Dark Rebirth — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/09/orc-incremental-review-an-incremental-auto-battler-inspired-by-classic-warcraft/)
- **Scales:** each draw raises the next draw's price. A commenter reported about 11.48e18 damage by level 1600 in the demo (reader comment, not the reviewer) — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/09/orc-incremental-review-an-incremental-auto-battler-inspired-by-classic-warcraft/)
- **Persists:** Blight spent on a 250-node tree, with node costs that jump steeply, such as a 10% bonus going from five to six figures in one tier — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/orc-incremental-review-a-great-horde-ruined-by-one-great-orc/)
- **Automated:** combat and offline gold. Live input is three spells — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/orc-incremental-review-a-great-horde-ruined-by-one-great-orc/). Bosses arrive every 10 levels — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4047270/orc_incremental.md)
- **Spike:** the rebirth cycle takes quite a long time and is not measured — [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/09/orc-incremental-review-an-incremental-auto-battler-inspired-by-classic-warcraft/)

#### Feed The Void
- **Repeats:** play cards to pass trials, earn tokens, upgrade the deck. Failing a trial keeps your progress, and the page stresses player control over luck — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4476320)
- **Scales:** cards level up with repeated use and gain "light". Numbers reach tens of thousands, with a perfect draw reported near a million — [itch.io comments](https://barribob.itch.io/feed-the-void/comments)
- **Persists:** relics, transient runes and unique decks unlock between runs — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4476320). Prestige resets the deck, and some players beat the game in only two prestiges — [itch.io comments](https://barribob.itch.io/feed-the-void/comments)
- **Automated:** nothing confirmed. Play is turn-based. **Session:** long or short, and it works in the background — [itch.io comments](https://barribob.itch.io/feed-the-void/comments)

#### Retromine
- **Repeats:** play resource, tool and action cards to mine, then escape before the cave collapses. Ore buys cards and upgrades between runs, and scorium keeps you alive — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4061320)
- **Scales:** ore totals reach above 10,000. Players report a 10-card hand limit in which a full hand can block cards you need, and bad opening draws can decide a run — [itch.io](https://cicidigital.itch.io/retromine)

#### Mine Cards
- **Repeats:** pickaxes auto-mine a card every 1.5, 1.25 or 1 second by tier. You buy cards, Stairs descend one level, the goal is level 100 — [itch.io](https://rose11.itch.io/mine-cards)
- **Scales:** Upgrade cards improve the top of the discard pile, such as 1 Stone to 2 Stone. Dynamite thins the deck — [itch.io](https://rose11.itch.io/mine-cards)

#### Nodebuster
- **Repeats:** destroy nodes with automatic area attacks that follow the cursor, spend on a tree. Bosses unlock new levels and prestige raises enemies and rewards. **Session:** 2 to 7 hours total, often one sitting — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3107330/nodebuster.md)
- **Automated:** early play requires aiming, and later stages become nearly passive — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3107330/nodebuster.md)

#### Ballionaire
- **Repeats:** place triggers, drop balls, watch them cascade, then meet a rising target. 145+ triggers and 55+ boons. "Autobonker" items spawn balls without input. 4x speed and an item ban are available. **Session:** about an hour, 10 to 15 hours for most content — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2667120/ballionaire.md)

#### Wildfrost
- **Repeats:** turn-based battles on a grid. Each card has a counter and acts when it hits zero, and you can buff, stall or speed up timers and reposition units. **Session:** 30 to 60 minutes per run. **Persists:** a hub town expands to unlock cards and tribes, and past winning teams can return as bosses — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1811990/wildfrost.md)

#### BALL x PIT
- **Repeats:** short brick-breaker runs that fund a base between dives. Base building has automated tasks for heroes at home that keep resources flowing. **Length:** about 24 hours for story, 30 for 100% — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2062430/ball_x_pit.md)

#### Zet Zillions (mechanic note)
- The starting deck holds Junk, which provides armour, and Trash, which is literally launched as ammunition. You can fuse cards in hand at any time, and an artillery cannon fused with Junk gives repeated hits — [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/one-of-the-craziest-roguelite-deckbuilders-zet-zillions-impressions/)
- Story chapters act as checkpoints, so failure is softened — [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/one-of-the-craziest-roguelite-deckbuilders-zet-zillions-impressions/). I did not confirm how large its numbers get.

### Inferences
**Number ceilings seen in normal play:** Feed The Void around 1 million, Retromine around 10,000+, Balatro's main game 50,000. Chef Knight reaches quadrillions in 3 hours, and Orc Incremental reportedly reaches e18 in a demo. In the sources checked, the very large figures come from games whose power keeps growing across resets rather than within one run.

**Specific mechanics worth borrowing, by source** (mechanics only, not a design):
- **Junk with a job:** Zet Zillions' Trash as ammunition and Junk as armour is the most direct precedent for clogging cards being needed, and fusing gives them a second use.
- **Auto-draw that can be upgraded:** Mine Cards' draw interval (1.5 s to 1 s) turns deck cycling itself into a purchasable speed.
- **Deck events as triggers:** Grail ties statuses to discard, shuffle and energy gain, which makes cycling and thin or fat decks strategic. Its 2-turn reshuffle penalty puts a price on an empty deck.
- **Timer manipulation verbs:** The Bazaar's Haste, Slow, Charge and Freeze, in particular Charge filling part of a timer instantly, are the nearest existing vocabulary for "feeding" a device.
- **Visible countdowns on both sides:** Wildfrost lets players plan around when enemies fire, and delay them.
- **Positional scoring order:** Balatro's left-to-right Joker resolution shows that order alone can be a satisfying decision, including the +Mult before xMult lesson.
- **Short fights with a decision-heavy shop:** Backpack Battles' 10-to-20-second battle length is a proven rhythm. Sell, reroll and adjacent-merge are its main tools.
- **Failure that still pays:** Feed The Void keeps progress on a failed trial, and Zet Zillions uses checkpoints.
- **Shrinking then accelerating targets:** Balatro's table shows a finite main game with gentle steps and an endless mode built to outrun any build.
- **Quota gates with a visible meter:** Chef Knight's corner indicator, time-as-reward and kitchen quotas that unlock floors.
- **Readable loss causes:** KeenGamer describes Orc Incremental's failures as easy to read, tracing every wipe to one of four causes.
- **Pacing tools expected at launch:** 4x speed (Ballionaire), adjustable battle speed (Grail), undo (Slice & Dice, Monster Train 2) and item bans (Ballionaire).

### Gaps
- No source measures upgrade cadence in minutes for any game, so the user's "burst of power every 1 to 5 minutes" has no direct verified comparison here. The pacing researcher may cover this.
- Developer postmortems and GDC talks on these hybrids were not found in this pass. I found no LocalThunk design interview about scoring.
- Whether enemies in Feed The Void deal damage to the player is unclear from the pages read. The store speaks of "trials" and commenters speak of combat rounds.

## Which games use a persistent board or automatically triggered tableau, and what decisions remain?

### Takeaway
Two families exist. Pure auto-boards (The Bazaar, Backpack Battles, Grail, Ballionaire, Orc Incremental) push nearly all decisions into shop, placement and draft stages between fights. Hand-plus-board games (Wildfrost, Monster Train 2) keep live decisions but are not incremental, and I found no game where a live hand feeds an auto-firing board within an incremental structure.

### Inferences
- Once fights run themselves, the game *is* the shop and the placement. Where the shop's choices collapse (see Orc Incremental below), nothing else is left to hold interest.
- Rebirth timing appears as a real decision in Orc Incremental. It is one of the few decisions native to incrementals, and it has no equivalent in the roguelike-style games here.
- Timed firing in existing games is driven by time (The Bazaar, Backpack Battles) or turn count (Wildfrost). A player-allocated resource that fills devices in a fixed order was not documented in anything I read, although The Bazaar's Charge effect is the closest.

### Gaps
- I did not verify how The Bazaar's board slots or item sizes currently work, because primary wiki and review pages were blocked or returned errors. Its values change between patches, for example [patch 13.0 in April 2026](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/patch-notes) (snippet-level).
- Whether Grail has anything persisting across fights other than the deck was not confirmed.

## What do reviews and forums say kills these hybrids?

### Takeaway
The strongest complaints are decisions collapsing into one answer, players turning into spectators, peak moments that perform badly or become unreadable, stalls after resets, and loss of tension once power runs away. I did not find many explicit "numbers feel fake" complaints: the nearest version is players saying gains are cancelled by costs, or that upgrades have no visible effect.

### Cited Findings

#### Decisions becoming irrelevant, solved builds
- Orc Incremental is the clearest case: a large share of upgrades funnels into the starting orc, the upgrades that make other units viable sit behind the most expensive nodes, a legendary uses several unit-cap slots while a basic orc uses one, tests of formation then die in seconds, and every choice points to the same answer — [KeenGamer](https://www.keengamer.com/articles/reviews/orc-incremental-review-a-great-horde-ruined-by-one-great-orc/). Balance is its top player complaint at 0.47, narrow upgrades 0.19 — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/4047270/orc_incremental.md)
- Balatro at high stakes: only a few builds, such as pairs and high card, are reliably viable (0.37) — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2379780/balatro.md)
- Backpack Battles: a stagnant meta where a few builds dominate — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2427700/backpack_battles.md)
- Ballionaire: some builds far outperform others (0.20), repetitive once strong combos are found (0.11), too many meaningless choices (about 0.05) — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2667120/ballionaire.md)
- Grail: some decks go infinite on turn 1 (0.13) — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3597200/grail.md)

#### Becoming a spectator
- Nodebuster: late play becomes an AFK grind where bosses and resources lose relevance (0.58) — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/3107330/nodebuster.md). One critic stopped watching enemies late on and just collected coins, found the combat a chore in the last 45 minutes, and called the game more a demonstration of compulsion mechanics than a game. - Ballionaire: much of the time is spent watching balls settle, and this idle time contributes to boredom — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2667120/ballionaire.md)
- BALL x PIT: some feel the game plays itself as meta-progression takes over, and late decision-making drops off (0.04) — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2062430/ball_x_pit.md)
- CloverPit: lack of agency makes it too repetitive, per critics (snippet-level) — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=1121635), [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/game/19389/cloverpit/reviews)

### Inferences
- The most directly relevant warning is Orc Incremental, because it shares the user's combination of incremental scaling, cards and automatic fights. When bonuses all multiply one thing, large numbers erase the choice that gave the cards meaning.
- High positive ratings do not contradict these criticisms. Chef Knight and Nodebuster are both highly rated and end after a few hours, before most of these problems accumulate. Complaints of "too short" look much less damaging than complaints of grind or spectating.
- Short, cheap, finite games seem to escape the "predatory" charge. This is one critic's view, supported only indirectly by the review scores.
- RNG complaints are nearly universal in this space, and Retromine's reports of bad opening draws and full-hand blocking are the closest documented signal for junk-clogged hands specifically.

### Gaps
- r/incremental_games, r/roguelites and r/slaythespire could not be read directly, so forum sentiment is under-represented. Steam review summaries stand in for it.
- I found no review explicitly complaining that large numbers feel fake, and I cannot say whether that means it is rare or simply went unsurfaced.
- I found no source explaining why Card Storm Idle sits at 51%, so I cannot say whether free-to-play DLC or design is the cause.

## What gaps exist: what has nobody done that this idea would do?

### Takeaway
I found no game that combines all of the user's elements, and every element has an isolated precedent. The most distinctive parts appear to be a **live hand that allocates a resource into auto-firing persistent structures in a set order**, and **a card-based battler with enemy HP reaching trillions in normal play**. This is "none found", not proof that none exists.

### Inferences
Apparent open space:
- **Keeping a live hand.** Incremental auto-battlers remove in-fight play, and hand-driven battlers do not grow into large numbers. A hand that keeps mattering while the board grows would keep a decision point that spectator complaints suggest is missing. This is untested, not a proven advantage.
- **Resource allocation as the firing mechanic.** Firing that depends on what the player feeds in, rather than a timer, could tie automation directly to a card decision. I found no documented example.
- **Junk inside an incremental.** Junk-as-ammo exists and incremental decks exist, but I found no game that combines them.
- **A deck inside a stepped power curve.** Prestige in Feed The Void resets the deck and draws complaints, so how a deck should persist across steps looks unresolved in the games I read.

Why the space may be empty for a reason, based on the complaints above:
- A hand can lose its meaning once board numbers dwarf any single card. This is the same mechanism KeenGamer describes in Orc Incremental, where a chieftain becomes a rounding error.
- Multiplying firing events and large numbers invites the frame-rate and readability failures seen in Chef Knight and Ballionaire.
- Clogged hands combine two common complaints: RNG and bad draws. Retromine's hand-limit feedback is an early sign.

### Gaps
- A broad search for a deckbuilder with persistent structures that charge and fire from a resource returned no such game. This is a null result from one search, so it is only as strong as that search.
- Coverage of small Steam and itch.io titles is partial. Most of the 109 and 178 itch.io tag results were not inspected, and Steam tag intersections could not be read. Reel Brawl and Pacremental, in particular, were seen only as one-line listings.
- I did not research Star Realms or Dominion themselves, or Slay the Spire 2, since they were not in scope for this topic.
- Whether any game uses a resource-fed firing queue is a negative claim that this search cannot prove. A quick manual look through Steam's Deckbuilding plus Incremental tags would be a useful check.
